## 🌍 Twenty Years Later: The Post-Phoenix Era

When the first wave of the ChroniX outbreak hit, humanity expected total extinction. Twenty years later, the world didn't end—it mutated. The survivors who remain are no longer fighting day-to-day for mere survival; they are building enduring institutions, establishing trade routes, and defining the political, cultural, and biological future of a shattered planet.

In *Phoenix Blaze*, civilization twenty years post-outbreak is defined by a fragile, brutal equilibrium between three emergent societal pillars: **Conscious Undead Enclaves**, **Corporate Remnants**, and **Neo-Agrarian Communes**.

---

### 🏛️ The Three Pillars of Post-Phoenix Civilization

| Civilization Type | Governing Philosophy | Economic Base | Stance on Conscious Undead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Synthesis Pacts** | *“Memory is a bridge, not a barrier.”* Hybrid societies where humans and conscious undead coexist under strict quarantine and chemical stability laws. | Botanical monoculture, synthetic blood-filtering, and recovered ChroniX tech salvage. | **Integrated:** Conscious undead hold citizenship rights provided their bloodlust is legally suppressed. |
| **The Vanguard Bastions** | *“Purity through fire and protocol.”* Militarized remnants of state and corporate defense forces operating behind reinforced concrete walls. | Industrial manufacturing, slave labor camps, and heavily guarded agricultural zones. | **Hostile:** Zero tolerance. All undead—conscious or feral—are viewed as biological targets for eradication. |
| **The Feral Syndicates** | *“Strength to the apex predator.”* Tribal confederacies that reject both human laws and undead hiding, embracing the chaotic evolution of the wasteland. | Black-market trade, raiding, mutant fauna domestication, and high-potency strain distillation. | **Opportunistic:** Conscious undead are treated as warlords, mercenaries, or prey depending on their power. |

---

### 🌾 Economy, Agriculture & Botanical Trade

Twenty years of adaptation transformed the illegal cannabis labs of the early outbreak into the cornerstone of global civilization:

* **The Terpene Standard:** Traditional fiat currency collapsed decades ago. In the post-Phoenix era, currency is backed by **Stabilization Grade Tinctures**—purified, high-potency botanical extracts required to keep conscious undead from losing their minds to bloodlust.
* **Bioreactor Cities:** Former corporate skyscrapers have been gutted and converted into massive vertical hydroponic towers. Wheat, corn, and specialized medicinal strains grow under flickering grow-lights, guarded by automated defense turrets and human-undead labor cartels.
* **The Silt-Road Networks:** Caravan routes snake across the ruined highways. These trade routes are patrolled by heavily armed scouts who trade high-grade seeds, clean water, and mechanical parts in exchange for forged steel and rare pre-outbreak artifacts.

---

### 🎭 Culture, Religion & Philosophy

Time heals nothing; it only mythologizes. Two decades after the collapse, a rich cultural landscape has emerged from the ashes:

* **The Cult of the Second Birth:** A widespread religious movement teaching that zombification was a divine evolutionary crucible designed to strip away human vanity and leave only pure, conscious survival instinct.
* **Memory Preservationists:** Nomadic archivists who scour ruined pre-outbreak libraries, hard drives, and museums to record lost human history, art, and music before it rots away entirely.
* **The Grey Code:** An unwritten legal system governing disputes between human settlements and undead enclaves, establishing rules for territory rights, bloodlust incidents, and trade disputes.

Your conscious undead protagonist isn't just hiding in a basement anymore—they are a living historical anomaly capable of shifting the balance of power between rising empires.


# Civilization Simulation: The World Twenty Years After Phoenix

The biggest evolution of High and Undead / Phoenix Blaze is moving beyond a conventional zombie survival game into a living, breathing civilization simulation.

The central question is no longer simply:

"Can Phoenix survive the apocalypse?"

It becomes:

"What kind of civilization emerges twenty years after the world ends, and what happens when the creatures who destroyed humanity begin building a world of their own?"

Twenty years have passed since the outbreak. The old world is gone, but the world itself has not stopped evolving. Humanity has adapted, zombies have changed, new societies have formed, and the ruins of civilization have become the foundations of something entirely different.

Phoenix isn't just surviving in this world. Phoenix is living inside a civilization that continues to develop, whether the player participates or not.

## 4. Politics, laws, and government

Who makes the rules when the old governments disappear?

Different societies should experiment with different political structures:

- Democracies.
- Councils.
- Tribal leadership.
- Military governments.
- Monarchies.
- Corporate-controlled territories.
- Religious or ideological movements.
- Independent communes.
- Anarchist settlements.
- AI-assisted governance.
- Undead-led governments.
- Human-undead coalitions.

Every government has policies, leaders, supporters, opponents, and consequences.

For example:

A settlement introduces mandatory infection registration. Some humans support it because they fear another outbreak. Conscious undead consider it discrimination. Hybrid citizens become politically divided. The policy triggers protests, black-market identity documents, migration, and eventually a possible uprising.

The player can influence these developments, but the world should not wait for the player to make every decision.

## 12. The civilization simulation engine

This is the technical heart of the concept.

The game needs a persistent world simulation that connects population, resources, politics, economics, ecology, and history.

## Civilization simulation architecture

WORLD SIMULATION ENGINE

Persistent world state and time progression

Population

Births, deaths, migration

Economy

Production, trade, scarcity

Politics

Laws, leaders, power

Factions

Diplomacy, conflict, alliances

Ecology

Wildlife, disease, climate

Technology

Research, industry, infrastructure

Culture

Education, beliefs, traditions

History

Events, consequences, legacy

WORLD EVENT GENERATOR

Emergent events, crises, opportunities, and historical changes

PLAYER INTERACTION

Influence, participate, observe, build, negotiate, or intervene

A practical implementation would use:

1. A persistent world-state database.
2. Individual settlement and faction records.
3. Population simulation with demographic summaries.
4. Resource production and consumption calculations.
5. Economic and trade calculations.
6. Political and diplomatic relationship models.
7. Environmental and infection simulation.
8. Time-step processing for world events.
9. Event history and consequence tracking.
10. Save/load and world-version management.

Not every NPC needs to be simulated every second. The engine can use different levels of simulation:

- Active simulation: Detailed updates around the player.
- Regional simulation: Periodic updates for nearby settlements.
- Background simulation: Aggregated calculations for distant civilizations.
- Historical simulation: Recorded events and long-term consequences.

This keeps a massive world manageable without making it feel static.

### 1.3 Damage calculation

The basic damage calculation:

\\[ D = W \times M \times C \times L \\]

Where:

- \\(D\\) = final damage.
- \\(W\\) = weapon base damage.
- \\(M\\) = attack or skill multiplier.
- \\(C\\) = critical multiplier, normally 1.0 or 1.5.
- \\(L\\) = location multiplier.

Example hit-location multipliers:

| Hit location       | Multiplier |
| ------------------ | ---------- |
| Body               | 1.00x      |
| Arm                | 0.75x      |
| Leg                | 0.70x      |
| Head               | 1.75x      |
| Weak point         | 2.00x      |
| Armored weak point | 0.75x      |

Armor is applied after the raw damage calculation:

\\[ D\_{final} = \max(1,\ D - A \times P) \\]

Where \\(A\\) is armor value and \\(P\\) is the weapon's armor penetration factor.

For damage types using percentage resistance:

\\[ D\_{final}=D\_{raw}\times(1-R) \\]

Resistance is capped at 80% to prevent ordinary builds from becoming invulnerable.

## 1.4 Critical hit rules

| Rule                          | Starting value |
| ----------------------------- | -------------- |
| Base critical chance          | 5%             |
| Maximum critical chance       | 60%            |
| Base critical multiplier      | 1.50x          |
| Precision critical multiplier | 2.00x          |
| Headshot multiplier           | 1.75x          |
| Stealth attack bonus          | +50% damage    |
| Critical resistance cap       | 75%            |

Critical chance formula:

\\[ C = \min(60,\ C_b + C_s + C_e + C_m) \\]

Where:

- \\(C_b\\) = base critical chance.
- \\(C_s\\) = skill bonus.
- \\(C_e\\) = equipment bonus.
- \\(C_m\\) = temporary mutation or effect bonus.

Critical hits should be calculated separately from headshots so that the two effects can interact through a controlled multiplier cap.

## 2.4 Control-loss formula

Calculate a control-risk score:

\\[ R = \max(0,B-70) \\]

\\[ P = \min(95,\ R \times 2 + S + T - W) \\]

Where:

- \\(B\\) = bloodlust from 0 to 100.
- \\(S\\) = stress contribution.
- \\(T\\) = triggering environmental stimuli.
- \\(W\\) = self-control and willpower reduction.
- \\(P\\) = control-loss probability, expressed as a percentage.

A control check occurs every 10 seconds while bloodlust is 75 or higher.

Control-loss severity:

| Bloodlust | Possible behavior                      |
| --------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 75–84     | Delayed commands, aggressive responses |
| 85–94     | Impulsive movement, targeting fixation |
| 95–99     | Uncontrolled attacks become possible   |
| 100       | Full temporary instinct override       |

The player should retain opportunities to resist through skills, equipment, preparation, and decisions.

## 6.3 Dynamic pricing formula

\\[ P = B \times S \times D \times R \times F \\]

- \\(D\\) = demand multiplier.
- \\(R\\) = regional modifier.
- \\(F\\) = faction or vendor modifier.

## 6.5 Vendor inventory generation

A vendor's available quantity can be generated from:

\\[ Q = B_q \times S_m \times R_m \times T_m \\]

Where:

- \\(Q\\) = available quantity.
- \\(B_q\\) = vendor's base stock.
- \\(S_m\\) = settlement production multiplier.
- \\(R_m\\) = regional resource multiplier.
- \\(T_m\\) = time-based restock multiplier.

Example:

| Vendor            | Item           | Base quantity | Restock  |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ------------- | -------- |
| General trader    | Food           | 30            | 24 hours |
| General trader    | Water          | 40            | 12 hours |
| Weapons dealer    | Ammunition     | 100           | 48 hours |
| Medical supplier  | Medicine       | 15            | 72 hours |
| Mechanic          | Components     | 25            | 48 hours |
| Farmer            | Seeds          | 50            | 7 days   |
| Research merchant | Rare materials | 5             | 7 days   |

Stock should be influenced by actual supply chains, not only by a timer.

## 6.6 Supply generation

Each settlement has a production and consumption rate.

\\[ S\_{next}=S\_{current}+G-C-T-L \\]

Where:

- \\(S\_{current}\\) = current stored supply.
- \\(G\\) = generated supply.
- \\(C\\) = consumed supply.
- \\(T\\) = supply traded or transferred.
- \\(L\\) = losses from spoilage, damage, theft, or disasters.

## 5.3 Taming rules

Animal taming should be a progression system.

| Stage    | Trust | Behavior                  |
| -------- | ----- | ------------------------- |

Taming should depend on:

- Species temperament.
- Player behavior.
- Food preferences.
- Time spent together.
- Environmental safety.
- Existing bonds.
- Threat exposure.

Not every animal should be tamable. Large predators and certain mutated creatures may only be temporarily calmed, trained through rare story events, or remain permanently wild.

## 5.6 Breeding rules

Animal breeding requires:

- Compatible species.
- Compatible genetics.
- Appropriate age.
- Health.
- Adequate habitat.
- Appropriate environmental conditions.
- A successful breeding event.

Offspring inherit weighted parental traits, including speed, resilience, temperament, intelligence, appearance, and infection resistance.

Mutation inheritance should be rare and governed by the game's fictional infection genetics.

- Recruitment.

- Emigration.
- Infection.
- Transformation.
- Rescue.
- Captivity.
- Conflict.

A simplified population equation:

\\[ P\_{next}=P+B+I+R-D-E-M \\]

Where:

- \\(P\\) = current population.
- \\(B\\) = births.
- \\(I\\) = immigration.
- \\(R\\) = recruitment or rescued arrivals.
- \\(D\\) = deaths.
- \\(E\\) = emigration.
- \\(M\\) = people removed through other modeled population transitions.

The simulation should record transitions between human, infected, hybrid, and undead populations separately, rather than treating every transformation as a death.

## 9.4 Migration rules

Migration should be driven by push and pull factors.

Push factors:

- Famine.
- Water shortage.
- Disease.
- War.
- Unemployment.
- Political oppression.
- Infected population pressure.
- Infrastructure collapse.

Pull factors:

- Available food.
- Clean water.
- Security.
- Employment.
- Family connections.
- Political freedom.
- Medical facilities.
- Trade opportunities.

Example migration score:

\\[ M = 0.25S + 0.20E + 0.20F + 0.15W + 0.10T + 0.10R \\]

Where:

- \\(S\\) = security attractiveness.
- \\(E\\) = employment availability.
- \\(F\\) = food security.
- \\(W\\) = water security.
- \\(T\\) = trade access.
- \\(R\\) = relationship and family attraction.

Migration should be calculated alongside the destination's capacity, route danger, and faction policies.

## 9.6 Faction expansion and territory takeover

Territory takeover should be a multi-stage process.

1. Reconnaissance

   Faction scouts identify weak territory, resources, population, and defenses.
2. Influence

   The faction establishes trade, political relationships, propaganda, or local alliances.
3. Pressure

   The faction applies economic, diplomatic, or military pressure.
4. Conflict

   Armed conflict, negotiations, internal uprising, or voluntary surrender may occur.
5. Control transfer

   Ownership changes when the takeover conditions are satisfied.
6. Occupation and rebuilding

   New laws, taxes, patrols, supply systems, and infrastructure policies take effect.
7. Integration or resistance

   The population accepts, resists, revolts, migrates, or forms an underground movement.

### Territory control calculation

A proposed control score:

\\[ T = 0.25M + 0.20E + 0.15P + 0.15D + 0.15L + 0.10S \\]

Where:

- \\(M\\) = military power.
- \\(E\\) = economic influence.
- \\(P\\) = population support.
- \\(D\\) = diplomatic influence.
- \\(L\\) = local infrastructure.
- \\(S\\) = strategic advantage.

The control score should be compared with competing faction influence, not treated as an automatic conquest button.

## 9.7 Civilization simulation ticks

Use multiple simulation speeds.

| Simulation layer     | Update frequency   | Detail                            |
| -------------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Active player area   | 10–60 times/sec    | Full gameplay                     |
| Nearby regional area | Every 1–10 seconds | Detailed NPC and enemy activity   |
| Regional world       | Every 1–5 minutes  | Settlements and factions          |
| Distant world        | Every 1–6 hours    | Aggregated population and economy |
| Historical world     | Event-driven       | Permanent records                 |

The simulation must use elapsed world time and deterministic rules so that saving, loading, and offline progression do not produce inconsistent results.

## 12. The final design objective

The real ambition here is to make every content category feed another category.

- Blood samples unlock biological research.
- Biological research unlocks mutations.
- Mutations change character capabilities and social identity.
- Cannabis genetics create agricultural products and new commercial opportunities.
- Crafting supports settlement development.
- Animals provide companionship, resources, and ecological activity.
- Enemies affect regional security and population movement.
- Regions create resource dependencies.
- Resource dependencies create trade relationships.
- Trade relationships influence political alliances.
- Factions compete for territory.
- Territory ownership changes the world.
- The world records its history and continues evolving.

Phoenix Blaze should have two simultaneous games running inside it:

1. The personal survival RPG, where the player fights, crafts, explores, transforms, builds relationships, and decides what kind of being to become.
2. The civilization simulator, where thousands of lives, settlements, economies, factions, ecosystems, and political systems continue developing around that player.

That is the difference between a zombie survival game with a big map and a persistent post-apocalyptic world with its own history, economy, ecology, and evolving civilizations.

The ultimate question is no longer simply whether the player survives. It is what kind of world survives with them.


This gives the game a single balancing source of truth. The same values can power the HUD, combat calculations, NPC behavior, crafting, trade, faction simulation, and save data.

The most important design principle: Every number must have consequences beyond its own system. A high bloodlust score can affect a conversation. A damaged relationship can affect recruitment. A shortage of food can trigger migration. Migration can destabilize a faction. A faction conflict can close a trade route. A closed trade route can change weapon prices. That chain of consequences is what turns High and Undead from a collection of game mechanics into a living world.

## 2.4 Damage calculation

\\[ D\_{raw}=W \times A \times L \times C \\]

Where:

- \\(W\\) = weapon damage.
- \\(A\\) = attack multiplier.
- \\(L\\) = location multiplier.
- \\(C\\) = critical multiplier.

Final damage:

\\[ D\_{final}=\max(1,D\_{raw}-A_r)\times(1-R) \\]

Where:

- \\(A_r\\) = applicable armor reduction.
- \\(R\\) = applicable percentage resistance.

Use separate armor and resistance calculations by damage type.

## 3.1 Core concept

Bloodlust is the conflict between conscious thought and the player's evolving undead biology.

The player remains a conscious person throughout the transformation. The undead instincts become increasingly powerful, but the player can learn to control, redirect, or embrace them.

## 3.6 Control-loss model

Control loss should not happen randomly without warning.

A possible model:

\\[ R=\max(0,B-70) \\]

\\[ P=\min(95,\ 2R+S+T-W) \\]

Where:

- \\(B\\) = Bloodlust.
- \\(S\\) = Stress contribution.
- \\(T\\) = Environmental trigger contribution.
- \\(W\\) = Willpower and control bonuses.
- \\(P\\) = probability of a control event.

Perform checks every 10 seconds while bloodlust is 75 or higher.

## 3.7 What happens during loss of control?

The game should use graduated behavior rather than immediately taking away the player's character.

- Character becomes fixated on nearby blood sources.
- Dialogue choices become limited.
- The character may ignore certain commands.
- Companion proximity causes tension.
- Movement may automatically approach a stimulus.
- Attacks may become more aggressive.
- The player receives a brief opportunity to resist.
- Severe episodes can trigger a temporary override.

The player should always understand why control is being threatened.

| Pregnancy themes      | On / Off, if implemented                                |

The exact presentation and distribution settings must follow the target platform's content policies and applicable age restrictions.

## 8.3 Character relationship system

Each eligible adult NPC should have a relationship profile.

| Attribute           | Range | Meaning                       |
| ------------------- | ----- | ----------------------------- |
| Trust               | 0–100 | Confidence in player          |
| Affection           | 0–100 | Emotional attachment          |
| Attraction          | 0–100 | Romantic interest             |
| Respect             | 0–100 | Opinion of player             |
| Commitment          | 0–100 | Relationship dedication       |
| Compatibility       | 0–100 | Shared values and personality |
| Jealousy            | 0–100 | Relationship insecurity       |
| Relationship stress | 0–100 | Current conflict              |

These are separate values. High attraction does not automatically mean high trust or consent.

## 8.4 Relationship stages

| Stage             | Description                       |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Stranger          | No relationship                   |
| Acquaintance      | Basic familiarity                 |
| Friend            | Positive personal connection      |
| Close friend      | Strong emotional trust            |
| Romantic interest | Mutual attraction may develop     |
| Dating            | Established romantic relationship |
| Committed         | Long-term partnership             |
| Complicated       | Conflict or uncertainty           |
| Separated         | Relationship ended                |
| Reconciled        | Relationship restored             |

Relationships must be based on character-specific preferences and mutually chosen interactions, not a single generic affection meter.

## 8.5 Consent and boundaries

Consent must be an explicit part of the relationship system.

Rules:

- Intimacy requires mutual consent.
- Consent can be withdrawn.
- A refusal must be respected.
- High affection does not imply consent.
- Alcohol or fictional substances must not be used to bypass consent.
- Threats, coercion, captivity, or manipulation must not unlock sexual rewards.
- Characters can establish personal boundaries.
- The player can set boundaries too.
- Relationship consequences should be meaningful and persistent.

## 8.6 Relationship interactions

Examples of non-explicit adult interactions:

- Conversation.
- Compliments.
- Shared meals.
- Dancing.
- Spending time together.
- Gifts.
- Personal conversations.
- Sharing memories.
- Mutual affection.
- Kissing, where permitted by the game's content rules.
- Choosing to share accommodation.
- Fade-to-black intimacy.
- Long-term commitment.

Each interaction should have requirements, outcomes, and relationship effects.

## 8.7 Adult social locations

| Location            | Activities                              |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Community bar       | Conversation and social events          |
| Settlement lounge   | Socializing                             |
| Private quarters    | Personal relationships                  |
| Dance hall          | Community entertainment                 |
| Festival grounds    | Celebrations                            |
| Private residence   | Domestic relationships                  |
| Community gathering | Social bonds                            |
| Medical clinic      | Sexual health education and care themes |

## 8.8 Relationship consequences

Relationships should affect the larger game.

Examples:

- A companion may defend the player.
- A partner may object to reckless behavior.
- A breakup can affect morale.
- A trusted relationship may unlock personal quests.
- A character may leave a dangerous settlement.
- Family responsibilities may influence settlement decisions.
- Romantic partners can have independent goals and relationships.

NPCs should have their own agency. They should not exist solely as romance options.

## 8.9 Mature themes and transformation

The player's undead transformation creates additional relationship complications.

Possible themes:

- Fear of physical changes.
- Acceptance.
- Trust.
- Physical vulnerability.
- Social stigma.
- Fear of bloodlust.
- Concerns about safety.
- Relationship adaptation.
- Human-undead cultural conflict.

A partner's response should depend on their personality, knowledge, beliefs, experiences, and relationship history.

## 8.10 Content presentation

Use a dedicated presentation layer:

- Age-gated entry where appropriate.
- Content warnings.
- Player-controlled content filters.
- Fade-to-black transitions.
- Relationship summaries.
- Dialogue boundaries.
- Scene availability rules.
- Persistent relationship state.

The goal is a mature, emotionally grounded game, not a collection of disconnected adult scenes.

## 9.1 Core purpose

Stealth allows the player to avoid detection, bypass enemies, infiltrate locations, gather information, escape dangerous situations, and complete objectives without unnecessary combat.

Stealth should be a complete alternative playstyle, not merely crouching.

It should account for:

- Visibility.
- Noise.
- Scent.
- Light.
- Movement.
- Cover.
- Disguise.
- Environmental conditions.
- Enemy senses.
- Enemy intelligence.
- Distraction.
- Concealment.
- Social infiltration.
- Evidence and suspicion.

## 9.2 Detection channels

Enemies should have several detection systems.

| Detection type       | What it detects             |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Vision               | Visible characters          |
| Hearing              | Footsteps, impacts, weapons |
| Scent                | Biological presence         |
| Movement             | Motion and sudden activity  |
| Light                | Silhouette and illumination |
| Proximity            | Nearby presence             |
| Social observation   | Suspicious human behavior   |
| Biological detection | Undead or hybrid traits     |

Different enemy species should use different combinations.

A normal human may rely on sight and sound. A mutated hunter may rely heavily on scent and movement.

## 9.3 Visibility

Visibility should be affected by:

- Light level.
- Distance.
- Movement speed.
- Character silhouette.
- Clothing color.
- Cover.
- Weather.
- Vegetation.
- Environmental contrast.
- Enemy vision.

Suggested visibility multipliers:

| Condition        | Visibility modifier |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Full daylight    | 1.00x               |
| Cloudy daylight  | 0.85x               |
| Dusk             | 0.65x               |
| Night            | 0.40x               |
| Darkness         | 0.20x               |
| Dense vegetation | 0.50x               |
| Heavy rain       | 0.70x               |
| Fog              | 0.45x               |
| Full sprint      | 1.50x               |
| Crouching        | 0.65x               |

These are initial gameplay modifiers. Darkness should not make the player completely invisible.

## 9.4 Noise system

Every action should generate a sound value.

| Action             | Noise value |
| ------------------ | ----------- |
| Slow crouch        | 2           |
| Normal walking     | 8           |
| Fast walking       | 12          |
| Sprinting          | 25          |
| Jumping            | 20          |
| Landing heavily    | 30          |
| Opening quiet door | 5           |
| Breaking door      | 65          |
| Melee impact       | 20–45       |
| Gunshot            | 80–100      |
| Vehicle engine     | 70–100      |

Noise should propagate through the environment.

Factors:

- Distance.
- Walls.
- Doors.
- Terrain.
- Wind.
- Weather.
- Indoor or outdoor location.
- Soundproofing.
- Enemy hearing.

## 9.5 Detection formula

A simplified detection score:

\\[ D_s=V+N+S+L+M-C \\]

Where:

- \\(V\\) = visual detection.
- \\(N\\) = noise detection.
- \\(S\\) = scent detection.
- \\(L\\) = light exposure.
- \\(M\\) = movement exposure.
- \\(C\\) = concealment.

Each component should be weighted by enemy sensory capability.

Detection states:

| Detection score | State             |
| --------------- | ----------------- |
| 0–19            | Unaware           |
| 20–39           | Suspicious        |
| 40–59           | Investigating     |
| 60–79           | Alert             |
| 80–99           | Target identified |
| 100+            | Full detection    |

## 9.6 Stealth movement

Movement states:

- Standing.
- Walking.
- Crouching.
- Crawling.
- Leaning.
- Peeking.
- Hiding.
- Moving between cover.
- Climbing.
- Quiet interaction.

Each state should have:

- Speed.
- Noise.
- Visibility.
- Stamina cost.
- Animation.
- Detection modifier.

## 9.7 Cover and concealment

Cover protects against vision and potentially incoming attacks.

Types:

- Vegetation.
- Vehicles.
- Walls.
- Furniture.
- Debris.
- Buildings.
- Shadows.
- Containers.
- Structural openings.

Cover must have a concealment rating and physical geometry.

The player should not become invisible simply because they are standing near a wall.

## 9.8 Distraction system

Players should be able to redirect enemy attention.

Distraction objects:

- Thrown objects.
- Noise makers.
- Alarm systems.
- Radios.
- Environmental machinery.
- Decoy devices.
- Triggered environmental events.

Enemies should investigate the source, communicate with nearby allies, and eventually return to patrol if they find nothing.

## 9.9 Hiding system

Hiding places:

- Closets.
- Under beds.
- Vehicles.
- Storage containers.
- Tall vegetation.
- Ruined structures.
- Underground areas.
- Concealed rooms.

Hiding places should have:

- Entry animation.
- Exit animation.
- Visibility rating.
- Noise rating.
- Escape options.
- Capacity.
- Detection rules.
- Environmental limitations.

Hiding should not guarantee safety if an enemy has a strong scent or investigation ability.

## 9.10 Social stealth

Humans should react to the player's appearance and behavior.

Social stealth should include:

- Clothing and uniforms.
- Faction identification.
- Reputation.
- Suspicion.
- Restricted access.
- False credentials as fictional gameplay items.
- Dialogue checks.
- Disguises.
- Behavioral consistency.
- Witnesses.
- Security inspections.

The player's undead condition should make social infiltration more complicated.

For example, a human settlement may permit entry to a trusted hybrid character but deny entry to a visibly mutated stranger.

## 9.11 Stealth takedowns

Takedowns should have:

- Target eligibility.
- Position requirements.
- Noise.
- Duration.
- Stamina cost.
- Detection risk.
- Target resistance.
- Nonlethal and lethal options where appropriate.
- Animation states.
- Consequences.

Not every enemy should be eligible. A massive brute or heavily armored enemy should resist ordinary stealth takedowns.

## 9.12 Stealth progression

| Skill           | Benefit                            |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Silent Movement | Reduced footstep noise             |
| Concealment     | Improved hiding                    |
| Observation     | Improved enemy awareness           |
| Infiltration    | Improved restricted-area access    |
| Distraction     | Improved distraction effectiveness |
| Scent Control   | Reduced scent detection            |
| Night Movement  | Improved low-light movement        |
| Ambush          | Improved opening attacks           |
| Escape Artist   | Improved escape success            |

## 9.13 Stealth and bloodlust

This connection is especially important.

High bloodlust can make stealth harder:

- Increased fixation on blood sources.
- Less predictable movement.
- Greater risk of involuntary noise.
- Increased difficulty resisting nearby targets.
- Stronger scent awareness.

But it can also offer stealth-related benefits:

- Better tracking.
- Enhanced hearing.
- Improved detection of living targets.
- Ability to sense enemies through certain environmental conditions.

The player must decide whether to control the predator or use it.

# 10. How all nine systems connect

This is the most important part of the architecture.

The systems should not operate independently. They should exchange data through shared gameplay services.

## The gameplay ecosystem

GATHERING

Resources, salvage, food, biological samples

INVENTORY

Storage, weight, equipment, item condition

CRAFTING

Weapons, armor, tools, medicine, components

BUILDING

Safehouses, workshops, farms, settlements

COMBAT

Defense and conflict

STEALTH

Concealment and infiltration

BLOODLUST

Biology and instinct

TRAVEL

Exploration and discovery

WORLD AND CIVILIZATION SIMULATION

Population, factions, economy, relationships, territory and persistent consequences

## 10.1 Example gameplay chain

Imagine the player discovers an abandoned industrial building.

1. Travel to the building using a back road.
2. Use stealth to avoid a group of infected.
3. Enter through a damaged service entrance.
4. Search storage containers.
5. Gather metal, electronics, and mechanical components.
6. Store materials in the backpack.
7. Discover a rare biological sample.
8. Bloodlust increases after exposure to infected remains.
9. The player must control their instincts to avoid attracting attention.
10. Escape with the collected materials.
11. Return to the settlement.
12. Craft weapon upgrades and electrical components.
13. Use those components to construct a powered workshop.
14. Assign an NPC technician.
15. The workshop begins producing equipment.
16. The settlement becomes more defensible.
17. A neighboring faction notices the settlement's growing industrial capacity.
18. A trade opportunity or territorial conflict develops.

One expedition has affected nine systems and potentially changed the world.

## 11.1 Shared event system

Every system should communicate through standardized events.

Examples:

| Event                          | Trigger                      |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| `resource:gathered`            | Resource collected           |
| `item:created`                 | Crafting completed           |
| `item:equipped`                | Equipment changed            |
| `item:broken`                  | Item durability reaches zero |
| `combat:damage`                | Combat damage applied        |
| `combat:enemy_killed`          | Enemy defeated               |
| `bloodlust:changed`            | Bloodlust changes            |
| `bloodlust:override`           | Instinct override begins     |
| `building:completed`           | Structure completed          |
| `building:destroyed`           | Structure destroyed          |
| `travel:region_changed`        | Region transition            |
| `travel:encounter`             | Travel encounter begins      |
| `stealth:detected`             | Player discovered            |
| `relationship:changed`         | Relationship changes         |
| `settlement:resource_shortage` | Settlement supply shortage   |

This lets systems react without directly depending on one another's internal implementation.

# 12. Definition of done

A system should not be considered implemented simply because it has a menu or a button.

| System    | Minimum implementation requirement                               |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crafting  | Working recipes, resource consumption, outputs, stations, queues |
| Combat    | Damage, hit detection, weapons, armor, enemies, injuries         |
| Bloodlust | Separate meters, triggers, decay, effects, control checks        |
| Gathering | Resource nodes, interactions, yields, depletion, regeneration    |
| Building  | Placement, construction, collision, structural health, upgrades  |
| Inventory | Items, stacking, weight, equipment, transfers, persistence       |
| Travel    | Navigation, movement, routes, encounters, vehicles               |
| NSFW      | Adult character rules, consent, relationships, content controls  |
| Stealth   | Detection, noise, visibility, scent, hiding, enemy investigation |

Every system also needs:

- User interface.
- Audio and animation feedback.
- Save/load support.
- Error handling.
- Data validation.
- Balancing configuration.
- Accessibility.
- Responsive controls.
- Automated tests.

Important distinction: This is the detailed design specification, not a claim that these features are already implemented in the source code.

My recommended implementation order is to build the shared item and resource foundation first, then inventory, gathering, crafting, combat, bloodlust, building, stealth, travel, and finally the mature relationship layer. This creates a working foundation instead of nine disconnected feature demos.

The north star: Every action should consume something, produce something, change something, or create a consequence. That is how Phoenix Blaze becomes a genuinely interconnected survival RPG.
