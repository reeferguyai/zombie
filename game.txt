The protagonist’s emotional journey: Phoenix moves from shock and basic survival, to investigating what happened and learning to use her zombie abilities, then toward deciding what her changed identity means. Balancing action and strategy: Combat should be dangerous and relatively brief; the strategy comes from deciding whether to fight, hide, disguise yourself, or use tools and the environment to get through. Between encounters, players plan around crafting, shelter, supplies, and maintaining bloodlust and disguise.

Fresh combat mechanics: Combat should feel specific to playing a zombie. Interacting with the world: Players scavenge and gather by hand or with tools, then craft equipment and develop shelters. Resource quality can vary by biome, weather, time, and tools; crafting can support experimentation and discovered recipes. Progression and replayability: Different skill builds, blood mixtures, mutations, and character choices let players try different approaches on a new run. New Game+ can carry over selected discoveries or mutations while resetting story progress and world state.

Expansions or DLC: Additional regions, story chapters, characters, or quests fit the design, but no specific DLC roadmap is established yet. Prioritize a complete base-game arc before committing to expansions.

Choices and replayability: Branching relationships, faction loyalties, research decisions, and identity choices can lead to different endings. New Game+ can add dialogue that acknowledges previous major choices and endings.

Difficulty and challenge runs: Offer selectable modifiers such as No Cannabis, No Blood Mixing, Pacifist, Iron Phoenix/permadeath, and Flesh Only/no tools. Let players combine modifiers and earn titles or visual rewards for completing them.

Time-limited events: These aren’t specified in the notes. For a primarily single-player game, use optional seasonal scenarios or rotating challenges that can be replayed later, rather than time-limited rewards that punish players for missing a window.

Leaderboards and challenges: Challenge runs and achievements are already a better fit than competitive zombie-kill rankings. If leaderboards are added, make them opt-in and focus on speedruns, survival time, or challenge completion so they don’t reward disruptive play against story NPCs.

Cosmetic purchases: Keep paid items visual only—outfits, scars, tattoos, or alternate mutation appearances. Character looks and expression options can also be earned through story and challenges; don’t sell combat strength, resources, or survival advantages.

Mod support: The notes propose an exposed modding surface. Support community-made cosmetics, recipes, challenge modifiers, and—if technically practical—regions or quests, while keeping saves and core story content stable.

Randomized elements: Use a saved world seed to vary resource placements, wildlife, patrols, encounters, and minor locations between playthroughs. Keep story-critical locations and reveals consistent, so a new layout creates variety without making the narrative unreliable.

Soundtrack: Use sparse ambient music for exploration, with a dark, haunting palette that can turn tense or frightening. Lo-fi or cannabis-inspired textures could appear during specific moments or altered perceptions, but shouldn’t make the whole apocalypse feel lighthearted.

Atmosphere: Layer Phoenix’s movement with wind, weather, distant activity, and nonverbal zombie sounds. Use distance and environmental obstruction so a nearby moan feels different from one echoing across a street; spatially placed audio logs can also reward careful listening.

Dynamic music: Cross-fade between four states: calm exploration, tension, combat or chase, and uncontrolled horror. Increase percussion, drones, or melodic intensity as more enemies detect Phoenix or her bloodlust rises.

Voice acting: Phoenix’s voice can shift with her condition—more human when calm, rougher as bloodlust rises, and less verbal when uncontrolled. The notes also propose distinct voices for her inner perspectives and recognizable vocal signatures for conscious-undead characters. GanjaGuru isn’t identified in the current character notes; if that character is part of the cast, define their role and voice before choosing a performance style.

Ambient sounds: Make quiet moments informative: faint footsteps, rustling, distant voices, or zombie calls can suggest nearby danger. Weather and distance can obscure environmental sounds, encouraging players to pause, listen, and decide whether to move on.

Combat cues: Give attacks distinct, readable sounds—wind-up, impact, hit or miss, and enemy reaction—so players can respond even when the screen is crowded. Zombie sounds should convey detection, pursuit, or loss of target, complementing visual cues rather than replacing them.

Important music moments: Use short identity motifs for major mutations and story revelations, as proposed in the notes. Give major threats or boss-like encounters their own musical variation, but reserve those cues for genuinely significant moments so they stay memorable.

Cannabis audio feedback: Make consumption and strain effects audible as well as visible. The notes suggest that some strains or blood mixtures can temporarily alter Phoenix’s perception—for example, emphasizing nearby heartbeats or muting zombie sounds—while state changes should always receive clear feedback.

Stealth audio: Use footsteps, surface noise, and nearby enemy reactions to help players judge how exposed they are. A quiet floor, broken glass, and rain should sound and behave differently; suspicion cues should indicate when an enemy has heard something, without revealing its exact position automatically.

Overall soundscape: Let the contrast carry the tone: ruined-world ambience, body-horror vocal changes, moments of melancholy, and brief dry humor. Cannabis-related audio can express Phoenix’s perception and coping strategies without turning the soundtrack into a constant novelty cue.

### **12. Survival, Health & Failure**

Survival meters: The proposed meters are Health, Bloodlust, Decay, Stamina, Energy, Hydration, Body Temperature, Consciousness, Mental Stability, Human Suspicion, and Zombie Suspicion. They represent Phoenix’s physical condition, control, mental state, and risk of being detected; the design still needs to clarify how Energy, Consciousness, and Mental Stability differ mechanically.

Decay: As decay rises, it changes Phoenix’s appearance and can impair her movement and health, weaken her human disguise, and affect social interactions. Fictional treatments or cannabis effects can temporarily slow it.

Other survival needs: Yes. The design includes hydration, body temperature, and energy, plus a separate Fatigue meter. Fatigue rises with time awake, exertion, bloodlust, or injury; high fatigue reduces stamina and reaction speed, while weather affects temperature and movement.

Injuries and treatment: Phoenix can suffer bleeding, infection-related effects, broken limbs, movement impairment, and damage over time. Healing and recovery items are planned, but the specific treatments and recipes still need to be defined.

Dangerously high bloodlust: High bloodlust increases aggression, reduces control, makes disguises harder to maintain, and can trigger unwanted feeding. At 90 or above, it begins causing hunger damage; losing control in front of important NPCs can also seriously damage relationships or attract faction hostility.

Defeat and death: In standard mode, reaching zero health leads to a defeat screen where the player can load the last save or the last safehouse rest. The notes don’t impose extra progress loss beyond the player’s save timing. Optional Iron Phoenix permadeath mode makes death final for that save.

Safehouses: Safehouses can be secured and used to save, craft, and recover. 4. **Balance testing:** Track how often players are detected, lose control, run out of resources, or avoid combat. Adjust rates based on observed play, not assumptions.
5. **Release gate:** Require a stable build, working saves, clear controls, no blocking bugs, and complete core content before release. The notes already call for alpha testing and iteration. 

### **Accessibility, Ratings & Rights**

1. **Accessibility:** Include remappable controls, scalable text, colorblind options, screen-reader support where feasible, and combat assists. 
2. **Rating and warnings:** Target an adult rating and provide clear warnings for horror, gore, cannabis-related fictional content, and mature relationship themes.
3. **Mature-content settings:** Let players disable or limit mature content, and verify that each selected platform supports the intended presentation.
4. **Ownership:** Confirm ownership and license terms for code, art, writing, music, fonts, and third-party tools.
5. **Distribution rules:** Check each storefront’s current age-rating, content, privacy, and payment requirements before committing to launch there.

### **Business & Release Plan**

1. **Store:** For a PC-first version, choose a PC storefront and maintain a separate browser build only if it fits the technical plan.
2. **Price and model:** The notes consider a paid game with optional cosmetics or expansions and reject pay-to-win or paywalling core survival systems. Exact pricing is still open. 
3. **Launch format:** Prove the core loop in a prototype first; decide between a demo, Early Access, or full release after scope and production capacity are clearer.
4. **Store materials:** Prepare a clear description, screenshots, trailer, feature list, content warnings, and supported-platform details.
5. **Communicating scope:** Clearly distinguish what is playable at launch from ideas planned for later, so players aren’t promised unfinished systems.

### **Post-Launch Support**

1. **Support owner:** Assign a person or team to triage bug reports and player questions.
2. **Update cadence:** Set a realistic patch schedule based on team capacity; don’t promise frequent updates before knowing the workload.
3. **Save safety:** Version saves and test migrations before updates; back up saves where the platform allows it.
4. **Expansions and mods:** Consider new regions, chapters, and mod support after the core game is stable. These are proposed future options, not commitments.
5. **If support ends:** Communicate clearly, preserve the last stable version where possible, and avoid leaving saves dependent on unavailable online services.

### **Data, Privacy & Security**

1. **Data collection:** Collect only what the game needs; disclose optional crash reports or analytics and ask for consent where required.
2. **Offline access:** Keep the core game playable without an account or external service.
3. **Save protection:** Use versioned saves, backups, and clear recovery behavior if a save is damaged.
4. **Third-party records:** Maintain a list of external tools, libraries, services, and licenses.
5. **Privacy information:** Provide a plain-language privacy notice if any personal data, analytics, or online services are used.

### **Game Flow**

1. **Launch screen:** Show the title, content settings, accessibility options, and clear paths to start or continue.
2. **Start to play:** Move from title screen to character creation, then into the apartment-morning prologue.
3. **Session flow:** Let players pause, save where allowed, resume, return to the title, or load another save.
4. **After the story:** Offer New Game+, challenge modifiers, and continued exploration only if they can be supported without undermining the ending.

### **Camera & Controls**

1. **Camera:** Use a top-down camera for exploration and combat, with limited zoom or framing changes for important scenes.
2. **Movement and actions:** Use directional movement, contextual interaction, and quick inputs for attacks and survival items.
3. **Combat targeting:** Use facing-direction attacks for melee and cursor or aim controls for ranged options, with lock-on assist available.
4. **Control schemes:** Support keyboard and mouse first; add controller support for PC, and touch controls only if mobile becomes a launch target. The notes already propose keyboard and touch controls. 

### **Rules & Outcomes**

1. **Ordinary objective:** Survive, gather, craft, manage bloodlust and disguise, and pursue the next clue or objective.
2. **Defeat or loss of control:** Standard defeat returns the player to a save or safehouse rest; losing control can cause relationship and faction consequences. Optional permadeath can be a separate mode. 
3. **Mission failure:** Let optional quests fail or change when the world changes, but keep major story routes understandable and avoid invisible fail conditions.
4. **Objective completion:** Use a journal, map markers, dialogue, and clear completion feedback to show when an objective is done or an ending is reached.

### **Scenes & Build Requirements**

1. **First-build scenes:** Title/menu, character creation, apartment, apartment building/hallway, nearby street, temporary safehouse, inventory/crafting, and a basic defeat/save flow.
2. **Required content:** Phoenix, a few civilians, two or three enemy types, interactable household items, gathering points, one important clue, and a small neighborhood environment.
3. **Transitions:** Keep the apartment, hallway, street, and nearby interiors connected through short, readable transitions; stream areas where feasible.
4. **Build first:** Create the apartment-to-street opening first. It tests the protagonist, tone, controls, first reveal, and the key survival loop in one compact slice.

### **Player Feedback & Interface**

1. **Condition:** Keep Bloodlust, Decay, disguise strength, and quick-use slots visible; show stamina, sanity, and suspicion cues when relevant. 
2. **Interactable objects:** Use contextual prompts and distinct visual states for usable, gatherable, and examinable objects.
3. **Danger cues:** Give enemies readable animations and audio/visual signals when they notice, investigate, or pursue Phoenix.
4. **Teaching controls:** Teach through short contextual prompts and safe practice moments rather than pausing the game for long tutorials.

### **Saving & World State**

1. **Save locations:** Support manual saves, autosave, and quicksave; safehouses can also serve as reliable rest/save points. 
2. **Persistent state:** Save inventory, relationships, quest choices, recipes, buildings, crops, world seed, and important regional/faction changes.
3. **After defeat or story choices:** Loading a save restores the state from that point; warn players before choices that permanently alter relationships or routes.
4. **Preventing corruption:** Use versioned saves, backups, error handling, and never silently overwrite an existing save. 

### **First Playable Build**

1. **Smallest demonstration:** One continuous sequence from waking in the apartment to reaching a temporary safehouse after the first street encounter.
2. **Must work, not just appear:** Movement, interaction, basic stealth, bloodlust feedback, one gathering/crafting action, cannabis use, an enemy detection response, and saving.
3. **Tester’s task:** Start without instructions, discover the immediate objective, leave the apartment, respond to the street threat, reach safety, and explain what Phoenix is and what she needs to do next.
4. **Change the concept if:** Players don’t understand the zombie perspective, the opening reveal doesn’t land, or the core gather–hide–survive loop feels less interesting than a conventional combat game.
